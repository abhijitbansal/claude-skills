# motion.json reference

Validate with `node scripts/validate.mjs motion.json`. Unknown properties are errors (with did-you-mean). All coordinates are canvas pixels (origin top-left); an element's `x`,`y` is its **centre**.

### Variables
`vars` declares names (`^[a-z][a-zA-Z0-9_]*$`, up to 30; `index` is reserved for the `--out` row number), each `{ "type": "string" | "number", "default"? }` with a `default` of its own type. `{{name}}` in any string value of the spec (text, a counter `prefix`, a chart title, an image or audio `src`, a colour, a block param, an element `id`, even `mode` or `theme`) is replaced by the value before anything else reads the spec. A number becomes its plain text inside a longer string (`"Q{{n}}"` gives `"Q3"`), and a string that is exactly `{{n}}` of a `number` var becomes the number itself, so `"to": "{{count}}"` fills a counter's number; the substituted spec is then checked as usual, so `"text": "{{count}}"` alone is `must be string` (write `"{{count}} orders"`), and `"to": "{{name}}"` with a `string` var is `must be number`. Each var takes its `default`, or with `render --batch` the row's value. The substituted spec goes through every normal check, so a value that names a file outside the spec folder or a colour that is not one fails at the field it lands in, as if written there. A reference is exactly `{{name}}`: `{{ name }}` (with spaces) and `{{Name}}` stay as written. In a spec that declares `vars`, write `{{{{` for a literal `{{` (`"{{{{user}}"` shows `{{user}}`); a spec without `vars` is never searched, so `{{` in its code or text needs no escape. Errors: `scenes[0].elements[0].text: unknown variable "citty"; declared: city, name`; `vars.city: no default and the row gives no value`; `vars.seats.default: must be a number, as its type says (got a string)`. A value is inserted as it is and never searched again.

### Beats
`node scripts/beats.mjs <audio> [--out <file>.json] [--min-confidence 0.5] [--bpm <n> | --min-bpm <n> --max-bpm <n>]` decodes the audio with ffmpeg (the first five minutes of any file with an audio stream: mp3, wav, m4a, aac, ogg, flac, or a video; at least about 2.2 s), finds one tempo and lays a steady beat grid over the sound, then writes `<audio-stem>.beats.json` beside the audio (or `--out`, which must be a `.json` file that is new, empty or already a beats file, never the audio, a folder, a symbolic link or another JSON file such as the spec): `{ "source": "music.wav", "bpm": 120.08, "confidence": 0.88, "beats": [0.005, 0.505, …] }`, beats in seconds to the millisecond. It prints the path, then `120.08 BPM, 16 beats`.

What the detector does, and what it was measured on (synthetic drum-like audio; real music is not measured here):
- **Onsets.** Spectral flux (the rise of each frequency's magnitude, summed) of a 1024-sample window every 512 samples (23 ms) at 22050 Hz, each frequency weighted `1 / (1 + (f / 850 Hz)^2)`: a broadband hi-hat raises hundreds of high bins and a kick a few low ones, so unweighted the hats outweighed the kick and the grid sat on the off-beat (kick every beat with off-beat hats read right but half a beat late in all 18 test cases; weighted, it lands on the kick in all 18). Log-compressing the magnitudes instead was measured and made ghost notes and timbres worse.
- **Tempo.** The autocorrelation of the onsets, lightly smoothed (a 5-frame triangle: a sharp transient and its repeats rarely fall on whole frames), over 60 to 200 BPM. Each lag is scored by the autocorrelation at 1 to 4 times the lag (a pulse train, so a peak at 1.5 beats loses to the beat, whose multiples all carry onsets) and by a wide tempo prior (a log-normal bump at 120 BPM, 1 octave wide); the pick is then refined against the beat grid over longer and longer stretches of the track (a 300 s track keeps its grid to within 20 ms). Measured: click tracks from 60 to 200 BPM with 2, 5 and 20 ms transients all read within 0.2 BPM with every beat within 20 ms of a click; fast kicks over off-beat hats (156 to 180 BPM) and half-time grooves with 16th hats read at the beat or at half, never at 2/3 or 3/4 of it.
- **Octaves.** A pulse and its half or double often correlate alike. The candidates (the pick, its half and its double, each at least 0.3 as periodic as the strongest) are judged by evenness: the fastest one whose beats alternate no weaker than 0.9 of each other is the tempo (clicks at 180 or 200 BPM are the beat, where ghost notes between the beats of 90 BPM, at 0.75, are not); otherwise the most periodic under a narrower octave prior (centre 120 BPM, 0.6 octave on the slow side, 0.35 on the fast side) wins.
- **Beats.** A steady grid at that tempo on the phase that sits on the most onset, over the sound only: a frame is sound above a floor of 3 times the quiet 5th percentile of the frame level, never above a tenth of the loud 95th and never below -66 dBFS. A silent or hissing intro or outro has no beats; a quiet intro, a fade-in or a fade-out keeps its beats whatever the loudest hit is; a sustained pad or hum counts as sound, so the grid starts where it does; `beat:0` is the first beat inside the sound (a click on the very first samples counts). A silent stretch in the middle keeps the grid, so beat n stays the song's nth pulse.
- **Confidence.** How far the strongest autocorrelation peak (of the unsmoothed onsets) stands above the range's average, as a share of the peak (`1 - mean / peak`, 0 to 1), cut when the grid drifts (below). Measured: click tracks 0.85 and up, busy grooves (a kick on every beat with 16th-note hats at 120 and 128 BPM) 0.78 and up, white noise at most 0.35 (20 seeds at 4 s), 0.28 (20 at 8 s) and 0.12 (6 at 30 s); narration-like syllable bursts reach 0.58 and pass 0.5 in 8 of 30 seeds, so narration given by mistake can pass the gate (see Known limits). Under `--min-confidence` (default 0.5, a number from 0 to 1) nothing is written and the exit is 1: `no steady beat found in noise.wav (confidence 0.19; needs 0.5)`. A clip too short for two beats at the slowest tempo exits 1 with `x.wav is too short to find a beat (needs about 2.2 s of audio, this has 1.5 s)`.
- **Drift.** The grid's best phase is measured again in the first, middle and last third of the sound; if the thirds differ by more than 1/8 of a beat the confidence is cut to at most `0.4 * (1/8) / drift`, which fails the default gate, and the message says so: `no steady beat found at 121 BPM in x.wav (confidence 0.39; needs 0.5): the grid drifts 0.13 of a beat across the track; retry without --bpm, or with a tempo closer to the track's` (12 s of clicks at 120). Measured: a forced `--bpm` 1 to 4 % off fails on every track tried (100 BPM for 12 s, 96 for 60 s, 133 for 120 s, 111 for 30 s; 0.5 % off on 12 s drifts 0.07 and passes), and the right tempo drifts at most 0.01; a 100-then-130 BPM change fails (drift 0.20). A 120-then-90 change (drift 0.02) and a ritardando from 120 to 100 (drift 0.11) are not caught. For a tempo the detector found, the drift is named only when it is what put the confidence under the gate.
- **Forced tempo.** With `--bpm` the confidence is how well that grid fits the onsets, not the autocorrelation (which asks how alike the track is one beat apart; a syncopated pattern is not, even at its right tempo: bossa at 124 scored 0.09 that way). In each third of the sound, the onset level the typical beat of the grid reaches is set against chance, the same beats moved off the beat by 1/8 to 7/8 of a beat: their average on the plain spectral flux, their 75th percentile on the weighted envelope above (it rests on a few low bins, so noise moves it more). The better of the two counts, the worst third decides, and the drift cut applies. Measured: the right `--bpm` passes on 193 of 194 cases (the regression set, both held-out sets and a kick on 1 and 3 at 124 and 140) with every beat within 20 ms of the true beat, and 3 % either side fails on all 388; the one under the gate is a half-time groove at 144 with 16th hats (0.41; 0.49 before), whose beats 2 and 4 carry only the hats that every sixteenth has. So with `--bpm` the gate measures how well that tempo fits the onsets, not whether the audio has a beat: forced on non-rhythmic audio it passes now and then (a review run, 120 runs per source: speech-like syllable bursts 6 times, at most 0.73; a drone with slow chord changes 3 times, 0.63; rain twice, 0.51; pink and brown noise and applause never), mostly on clips of 3 to 8 s, and `--min-confidence` is no reliable guard there. Force a tempo only on music you know has that beat.
- **Held-out check.** 60 cases written by a reviewer who had not seen the tuning set (`test/beats-heldout.test.mjs`: off-grid tempos, waltz, 6/8, reggae, house, drum and bass, trap, noise under clicks; never tuned on): 57 of the 57 outside 6/8 read right (50 at the tempo, 7 at a half or double named below), and the grid is on the beat (within 1/8 of a beat) in all 50 exact-tempo cases; of the three 6/8 cases 2 read the dotted-quarter pulse and 1 the eighth notes (three times as fast). A second set of 31, written by a later review; the tempo estimator has not been tuned since (`test/beats-heldout2.test.mjs`: shuffle, disco, breakbeat, polka, march, bossa, soft and sharp transients, echo, room taps, crowd, crackle, pink noise, humanised timing): the tempo is missed in 4 of the 30 cases outside a documented octave (13.3 %), all of them known limits below (a polka at 134 reads 2/3, bossa at 124 and 138 reads half, rim clicks at 186 under pink noise are refused), and the grid is on the beat in all 26 exact-tempo cases.

Known limits, each with its fix. `--bpm` needs the exact tempo (1 % off already drifts and fails); when you know the tempo only roughly, give `--min-bpm` and `--max-bpm` from about 0.8 to 1.25 times the tempo you hear (`--min-bpm 107 --max-bpm 168` for a polka at about 134), a range that holds no half, double or 2/3 of it.
- Octaves that nothing in the audio settles. Below about 75 BPM a kick with ghost notes on the eighth notes reads at double (70 reads 140). A kick on 1 and 3 over weak hats on every beat reads at half from about 115 BPM with a soft kick (112 reads the beat, 120 to 148 read half) and from about 125 with a clickier kick (124 reads the beat, 128 reads 64); a rock groove at 150, 168 or 192 reads 75, 84 or 96. A half-time trap at 140 reads 70, which is defensible (drum and bass at 172 and 174 reads the beat). Fix: the range (`--min-bpm 112 --max-bpm 175` for a groove at about 140) or `--bpm`.
- A reggae one-drop (kick and rim on 3, chords on the "ands") reads at double: 70 and 76 read 140 and 152. Keep that read and anchor on every other beat (`beat:0`, `beat:2`, `beat:4`, …): `--bpm 70` and the range both read 70 at full confidence (1.00 and 0.93) but lock the grid half a beat late, on the off-beat chords, and no flag sets the phase.
- A half-time groove with 16th hats (kick 1, snare 3) reads at half: a trap at 140 reads 70. Fix: `--bpm` only, never the range, which can lock confidently on a tempo no beat of the groove has (132 read 110 at 0.72 and 144 read 120.1 at 0.73, 5/6; with a guess 10 % off, 3/4 or 4/3 at up to 0.80). At some tempos the right `--bpm` sits just under the gate (144: 0.41 and 0.49 on two syntheses; 140: 0.58 and 0.71; 132: 0.93), so give `--bpm 144 --min-confidence 0.4`: a `--bpm` 3 % off scored at most 0.10 there.
- Latin syncopation (bossa) reads at half from about 124 BPM (124, 138 and 150 read 62, 69 and 75), at 2/3 at 112 (74.7) and at double at 100 (199.9). Fix: the range (`--min-bpm 99 --max-bpm 155` for bossa at about 124) or `--bpm 124` (confidence 0.72 to 0.88 on bossa at 100 to 150).
- A 2-beat oom-pah (polka) can read at 2/3 speed: 126 and 134 with a snare on the "ands" read 84 and 89.3, 140 reads 93.3, 150 reads 100. Fix: the range (`--min-bpm 107 --max-bpm 168` at about 134) or `--bpm 134` (0.66 to 0.79).
- Compound meters (6/8 and 12/8): the pulse may read at the dotted quarter or at the eighth notes (a 6/8 at a dotted quarter of 50 read 150, its eighths). Fix: `--bpm 50` (0.97) or `--min-bpm 40 --max-bpm 63`.
- Tempos below 60 BPM are outside the default search: clicks at 45 and 50 give nothing and 55 reads 61.8 and is refused. Fix: `--bpm 50` or `--min-bpm 40 --max-bpm 63`.
- Sparse rim clicks under heavy noise are refused by the gate, not misread: rim clicks at 186 under pink noise 0.3 read 124 at confidence 0.10, and at 150 under pink noise 0.2 the right tempo reads at 0.40. A range does not help (0.06 to 0.11 on the 186 track). Fix: `--bpm 186` (0.88, every beat within 4 ms); `--bpm` passes on all 5 rim cases measured under pink noise 0.2 and 0.3 (0.84 to 0.94).
- One tempo for the whole track. A tempo change from 100 to 130 fails the gate (drift 0.20), but 120 then 90 reads 119.9 at 0.84 and a ritardando from 120 to 100 reads 115.7 at 0.87: they pass, and the grid fits only part of the track. No flag fixes this: `--bpm` at either tempo, or anywhere in the ritardando, is refused (0.00), and a range around 120 reads 120 again while one around 90 is refused (0.20). Time the cuts in the other part by hand (`t` and `at` in seconds), or cut the audio at the change and use the part you need.
- Swing. A quarter-note swing of 40 ms reads 119.8 at 0.80 (`--bpm 120`: 1.00, beats within 47 ms of the swung hits). From about 70 ms it reads at half (60 at 0.93), its beats on the straight hits only: keep the half read. The range is refused (0.00). `--bpm 120` depends on the material: on one synthesis it passes at 1.00 with its grid on the late (swung) hits, so the straight hits sit 70 ms before their beats; on another it is refused (0.00). Eighth-note swing is not seen at all.
- Narration given by mistake can pass the default gate (syllable bursts: 0.58 at most, 8 of 30 seeds at 0.5 or more). Fix: `--min-confidence 0.6` refuses all 30, and every right read in the measured sets (189 cases) is 0.68 or more.

The tempo override: `--bpm <n>` forces the grid at n beats per minute (30 to 300) on the best phase; the confidence is then how well that grid fits the onsets (Forced tempo, above) and how little it drifts, so a wrong tempo fails the gate (`no steady beat found at 90 BPM in x.wav (confidence 0.00; needs 0.5) …`), and so does one that is only 1 to 4 % off (the drift message above); `--min-confidence` lowers the gate. `--min-bpm` and `--max-bpm` (30 to 300, the minimum below the maximum; the search defaults to 60 to 200) narrow the search, and a failure names the range (`no steady beat found between 100 and 101 BPM in x.wav (confidence 0.00; needs 0.5): no beat in that range; the track may be faster or slower`); `--bpm` cannot be combined with them, and one alone that leaves no range says what the other defaults to (`--max-bpm (50) must be above --min-bpm, which defaults to 60; pass a lower --min-bpm too`). A value that is not a plain decimal number in range, or an unknown option, exits 2 and lists what is accepted: `--bpm must be a number of beats per minute from 30 to 300 (got "abc")`.

The root `beats` names that file (a path inside the spec folder, resolved like every asset: no URL, no absolute path, no hidden file, no symbolic link out of the folder; a `.json` file of at most 1 MiB). A keyframe `t` (element keyframes at any group depth, and a scene camera's) or an audio, cue or captions `at` may then be the string `"beat:<n>"`, n a whole number from 0 (`beats[n]`):
- a keyframe `t` is the beat's time minus its scene's start; it must lie inside the scene: `beat:12 is at 6.0s, outside scene "two" (4s to 5.5s)`;
- an `at` is `beats[n]` in video seconds, or `beats[n]` minus the scene's start when the item names a `scene` (and must lie inside it, the same error);
- an index past the end is `beat:40 does not exist; the file has 16 beats`; a `beat:` with no `beats` file is `"beat:3" needs a root "beats" file`; any other `beat:` text is `"beat:two" is not a beat reference; write "beat:<n>" …`.

Beats resolve right after vars (so `"beat:{{n}}"` works; a var is inserted as text here, and the whole value stays one string) and before viral mode, blocks, theme presets and auto cues: validate, preview (which serves the resolved spec), check, render, the contact sheet and each `--batch` row (after its own substitution) all see plain seconds, and the frame-grid cut-off checks and the audits judge the resolved times. The resolved spec keeps `beats`, so the preview reloads when the beats file changes and `render --out` refuses to overwrite it. A written `cues` list (even with beat-anchored cues) is never added to: viral mode derives auto cues only when the spec writes none.

The beats file is read as untrusted input: an object whose `beats` is a non-empty array of up to 10000 finite numbers, 0 or more, in ascending order (equal beats are allowed); `bpm` (above 0) and `confidence` (0 to 1) are numbers when present. Anything else is an error at `beats` that names the file and the entry (`data/music.beats.json: beats[2] (0.5) comes before beats[1] (1); list the beats in ascending order`).

### Viral mode
`"mode": "viral"` sets defaults and adds warnings for phone-first short-form video; every rule is a default or a `check.mjs` warning, never a failure.
- `viral.safeZone` must leave room: left + right at most 720, top at most 758 (240 px above the captions at y 998), bottom at most 762 (160 px of captions); the validator names the limit.
- Defaults: `format` `9:16` (1080x1920) when neither `size` nor `format` is written; `loudness` −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP; `viral.safeZone` the union of the platforms' UI bands (top 290, bottom 675, left 120, right 195 px at 1080x1920). Captions sit in a lifted zone between the bands (from 52 % of the height down to the bottom band: y 998–1245, x 120–885 at 1080x1920); keep other text inside the safe zone and above y 998.
- The hook: in the first scene, a `"role": "title"` text without keyframes is on screen from frame 0 (opacity 1, scale 1.08 settling to 1 over 0.4 s with the `enter` ease) instead of the theme entrance, and still takes the theme's exit with `"exit": true`; not inside a group that is hidden at t 0 (it keeps the theme entrance there). A block opening the video shows its hook parts the same way: the `title-card` title, the `cta-close` headline, the `quote-card` quote and its card, the `listicle` title. A `story-beats` block always starts with its hook on (the same entrance, in any scene or mode). Later scenes keep the theme entrance.
- Auto cues: with no `cues` key, a `swoosh` (volume 0.6) on every cut, a `pop` (0.35) on every entrance (a first `opacity`, `draw` or `reveal` keyframe at 0) and a `ding` (0.5) on every counter landing, at least 0.4 s apart (one per cut) and at most 30. `"cues": []` turns them off; a written list replaces them.
- Warnings (`warning: viral: …` for the spec, `warning: <element path>: viral: …` for an element): a format other than 9:16; `audio` without `captions`; no text at frame 0, or a largest text at frame 0 outside 3–7 words; a held text or counter under a platform band (`text "cta" sits under the platform UI (bottom 675 px) at 11.00s`); nothing changing for more than 3 s where the dead-stretch audit looks away (the final 3 s and `hold` elements); with `viral.loop`, a last frame that differs from the first by more than 0.12 (mean per-channel difference). Without a browser, the format, captions and change-gap warnings (read from the spec's keyframes, tweens and captions files) still print.
- Evidence: the hook window and safe zones come from platform ad specs and creator guidance; −14 LUFS is a defensible default, no platform publishes one. Treat all of these numbers as tunable defaults, not rules.

### theme
Without `theme` the spec draws as `dusk` (`render --theme` can pick another for a spec that names none). The theme's **dark** colours and fonts become the defaults: `background` is its page colour, text is its text colour, shapes without a `fill` or `stroke` use its accent, charts take its palette and axis colours, `sans` text uses its body font, `"font": "mono"` its mono font and `"role": "title"` text its display font. A weight the theme font does not ship is mapped to the nearest one it does (no synthetic bold), mono default included. Anything the spec sets itself (`background`, `color`, `fill`, a chart `config`) still wins. Fonts come from the skill's bundled `shared/fonts`, no network. An unknown name is an error with a did-you-mean.

## Scene
`id` (string, globally unique), `duration` (0.2–60 s), `elements` (array), optional `camera` (see Camera below), optional `background` (a CSS colour, or `"art"`: the theme's seeded art, drawn full-bleed behind the elements in dark mode at the canvas size and seeded by the scene `id`, so the same spec always gets the same art; a light page-colour scrim dims it and text and counters on it get a soft page-colour halo), `transition` (`none` default | `fade`: scene opacity 0→1 over min(0.4 s unless `duration` is set, scene length), from the page background | `crossfade`: the same fade-in, over the previous scene, which stays on screen at full opacity until the window ends (so the stage colour never shows through; the previous scene is not faded out) | `push`, `slide`, `wipe`, `zoom`, `blur`, `iris`; or the object form `{ "type", "direction"?, "duration"? }`: see Transitions below). On the first scene every name but `none` and `fade` is an error, as there is nothing to come in over. `fade` on scene 1 makes frame 0, the video's thumbnail, blank.
Scenes play sequentially; scene *n* starts at the sum of earlier durations. Only the active scene is drawn, except during the window of a `crossfade`, `push`, `slide`, `wipe`, `zoom`, `blur` or `iris` scene (its `duration`, 0.4 s unless set, at most the scene's own length), when the previous one is drawn too (beneath at full opacity, or beside it for a `push`). Timing and audio never change: a transition overlaps the pictures, not the clock.

### Transitions

A scene's `transition` (and the root default) is a name, or `{ "type": "push", "direction": "up", "duration": 0.6 }`; only `type` is required. `duration` is 0.1–2 s (default 0.4), cut short to the scene's own length. The progress through the window is eased (`inOutCubic`); the outgoing scene stays mounted until the window ends. During a `push`, `slide`, `wipe`, `zoom`, `blur` or `iris` window both layers are painted opaque, with the scene's `background`, else the root one, so a scene that sets none still covers the old one (a `crossfade` keeps transparent layers on purpose). A scene background that is see-through (`transparent`, or any colour with alpha under 1: `rgba()`/`hsla()`, a slash alpha in `rgb()`/`hsl()`, or hex with alpha such as `#0008`) is painted over the root colour (and the theme's page colour if that is see-through too), so the old scene still cannot show. `check.mjs` counts every window (a `crossfade` too) as change, never part of a dead stretch, and does not judge contrast for text of the scene while it comes in, because the screenshot there is two layers; a text that shows only inside its window gets an `info:` line saying so. `direction` of `push`, `slide` and `wipe` is the edge the new scene comes from, `left` (default), `right`, `up` or `down`; of `zoom` and `iris` it is `in` (default) or `out`; `blur`, `fade` and `crossfade` take none, and an unknown direction is an error that lists the ones that type takes.

| `type` | what moves |
|---|---|
| `push` | the new scene enters from the edge and pushes the old one out the opposite side, both moving by the canvas size |
| `slide` | the new scene slides in over the old one, which stays put |
| `wipe` | the new scene is revealed by a rectangle growing from the edge |
| `zoom` | the new scene scales to 1 from 1.2 (`in`) or 0.8 (`out`) while it fades in |
| `blur` | the new scene sharpens from a 24 px blur to none while it fades in |
| `iris` | `in`: the new scene opens as a circle from the centre; `out`: the old scene closes to a circle over the new one |

The window is also where `check.mjs` looks for text over text. Use `references/examples/transitions.json` as the pattern.

### Camera

A scene's optional `camera` moves every element of that scene as one: `{ "keyframes": [{ "t", "zoom"?, "x"?, "y"?, "rotate"?, "ease"? }], "shake"?: { "amount", "hz"?, "seed"? } }`. A camera needs a keyframe that sets `zoom`, `x`, `y` or `rotate` away from rest (zoom 1, x 0, y 0, rotate 0), or a `shake` with an `amount` above 0; one that never leaves rest does nothing and is an error.

| field | type | default | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `keyframes[].t` | number ≥ 0 | required | scene seconds, sorted, at most the scene's `duration` (the rules for an element's keyframes) |
| `keyframes[].zoom` | number 0.5–3 | 1 | 1 is no zoom, 2 doubles the size, 0.5 halves it |
| `keyframes[].x`, `.y` | number (px) | 0 | the canvas point the camera looks at, measured from the centre: `x: 100` moves the picture 100 px left (times the zoom); with a `rotate` the pan turns with the roll, so the point lands off centre |
| `keyframes[].rotate` | number (degrees) | 0 | the picture rolls about the canvas centre (before the pan moves it) |
| `keyframes[].ease` | an ease name | `linear` | the ease of the tween that ends at this keyframe |
| `shake.amount` | number 0–100 (px) | required | the largest offset in either direction; the shake runs for the whole scene, so put it on a short scene of its own |
| `shake.hz` | number > 0, at most 60 | 8 | noise steps per second; the offset blends between steps |
| `shake.seed` | integer 0–7918 | 1 | the same seed always shakes the same way and every seed another way (the bound keeps the x and y noise of different seeds apart), so renders stay byte-identical |

A property is tweened between the keyframes that set it and held before the first and after the last, as for an element. The picture is `translate(shake) scale(zoom) translate(-x, -y) rotate(rotate)` about the canvas centre, drawn inside the scene's layer: the scene's background (a colour, or the `"art"` plate) stays on the layer and does not move, so zooming out below 1, panning, rolling or shaking never shows a gap, and a transition still moves, clips or fades the layer as a whole. The camera moves the elements only; the background, captions, grain and the vignette stay put. Use `references/examples/camera-push-in.json` as the pattern.

`check.mjs` measures boxes after the camera, so text pushed out of the safe area by a held zoom is reported (`text "top" leaves the safe area (top) at 3.97s`). While a camera tween runs, every element of the scene counts as moving for the safe-area, overlap and caption-zone rules (as if its own keyframe were tweening), exactly as an element's own tween does: the move is not judged while it runs (so a reveal pan that starts with a label part-way in frame, or a push out from a tight zoom, is fine), the pose it settles in and the scene's last frame are. Two keyframes with equal values are a hold, not a move, so a camera held that way is judged at every moment. A shake has no keyframes and does not count as moving, so keep text clear of the edges by `shake.amount` too. The text floor reads the size as drawn, camera zoom included, and is not relaxed by a camera tween. A camera tween or a shake is change for the dead-stretch audit (read from the camera node's transform at every step, so it also counts for a scene that shows no element, such as an art plate alone, and a camera held with equal keyframes is still a dead stretch) and the viral 3 s rule (which, reading the spec alone, ignores moves between equal values too).

|---|---|---|---|

## Texture
Optional root `texture`: `vignette` (boolean, default off) darkens the corners with one radial-gradient layer over the scenes (clear to 55 % of the way out, 0.55 black at the corners); `grain` (0–0.15, default 0 = off) lays one static layer of monochrome film grain over each scene, as an SVG `feTurbulence` seeded from the scene `id` and never re-seeded, so the same spec renders byte-identical frames. It is blended with `overlay` and its noise averages mid-grey, so it adds texture without changing a frame's average brightness (measured: the mean luma of a frame stays within 1 of the grain-free frame at `grain` 0.15 in dusk, paper and press). A higher `grain` is stronger. Unknown keys are errors with a did-you-mean. Specs without `texture` render as before.
**Cost, measured:** the render loop (seek plus PNG screenshot, 1920x1080, 90 frames, Playwright Chromium on one Apple-silicon Mac, two rounds) took about 17 to 33 ms per frame with no texture, about 42 ms with `vignette`, about 100 ms with `grain` and about 187 ms with both. A 30 s clip at 30 fps (900 frames) therefore costs about 1 minute more with grain and about 2.5 minutes more with both. Texture applies to every scene; leave it off for long renders you do not need it on. Each scene carries its own full-frame grain bitmap (one per scene at the canvas size), so a spec with very many scenes at 4K holds a lot of memory in the page; keep `grain` for short or few-scene clips there.

## Cues
Optional root `cues` array, up to 30: short sounds the renderer synthesises, no file needed, or a recorded one from the bundled pack (`kenney:<alias>`, below). Opt-in: a spec with no `cues` renders exactly as before, and a spec with neither `audio` nor `cues` stays silent (except in viral mode, which places cues itself unless `cues` is written; see Viral mode). Cues are mixed through the same graph as `audio` tracks; contact sheets and the preview ignore them.
| field | type | default | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cue` | `pop` \| `click` \| `whoosh` \| `chime` \| `impact` \| `riser` \| `swoosh` \| `typing` \| `drop` \| `ding`, or `kenney:<alias>` (see Recorded cues below) | required | `pop`: a 0.16 s blip with a bright tick at its start; `click`: a 0.04 s dry UI tick; `whoosh`: 0.8 s of air going past, loudest and brightest 0.4 s in (start it 0.4 s before the cut it marks); `chime`: a 1.4 s three-partial bell with a short room tail; `impact`: a 0.9 s thud whose pitch falls two octaves in its first 0.1 s; `riser`: 1.6 s of noise climbing in pitch and level, ending at full level (start it 1.6 s before the moment it builds to); `swoosh`: a 0.3 s whoosh for a transition, loudest 0.15 s in; `typing`: key clicks 0.035 s apart, one per character (0.035 s × characters + 0.05 s long); `drop`: a 0.6 s sub hit; `ding`: a 0.5 s notification ding. `whoosh`, `riser` and `swoosh` are widened above about 120 Hz (below it both channels carry the same sound, within about 1 % in level, since each channel is levelled to the same peak on its own); the rest are mono |
| `at` | seconds ≥ 0 | required (except with `follow`) | from the video start; with `scene`, from that scene's start. Must fall before the video end |
| `follow` | a `text` element `id` | none | `typing` only: the cue starts when that typing text starts typing (its scene start, or its first `value` keyframe) and clicks once per character. With `value` keyframes the clicks are spread evenly up to the first keyframe that reaches the whole text, so under an ease they only approximate the characters. Not with `at` or `scene`; counted at 0.035 s per character from where it starts typing, it must end inside the text's scene. Without `follow`, a `typing` cue is a single key click at `at` |
| `scene` | scene `id` | none | anchors `at` to the scene, as on an audio track, so changing an earlier scene's duration keeps the cue aligned |
| `volume` | 0–1 | 1 | literal gain on a cue that peaks at -6 dBFS (0.5); the mix is not normalised unless the spec has a `loudness` target or render gets `--loudness` |
| `pan` | -1..1 | 0 | equal-power: the centre is unity in both channels, a hard pan is 3 dB louder in its channel |
| `note` | `A`-`G`, optional `#` or `b`, octave 0-8 | per cue | tunes `pop` (default E5), `chime` (A5), `impact` (F2), `drop` (C2) and `ding` (E6); an error on `click`, `whoosh`, `riser`, `swoosh` and `typing`, which are noise |
**Recorded cues.** `"cue": "kenney:<alias>"` plays one of twelve recordings bundled with the skill (`assets/sfx/kenney/<alias>.wav`, 48 kHz mono, at most 1.5 s, from Kenney's Interface Sounds and Impact Sounds, CC0; sources in `assets/sfx/LICENSES/kenney.txt`). Aliases: `kenney:click`, `kenney:tick`, `kenney:switch`, `kenney:select`, `kenney:confirm`, `kenney:error`, `kenney:open`, `kenney:close`, `kenney:pluck`, `kenney:glass`, `kenney:thud`, `kenney:metal`. A recording goes through the same peak (0.5), `pan`, 5 ms fade and `volume` as a synthesised cue, so `volume` is still a literal gain; `note` and `follow` are errors on it (it has no pitch and does not type). Nothing is fetched at render time. Use a recording for a click or a hit, and a synthesised cue for a whoosh or a riser. The alias is looked up in this table, never used as a path, so `kenney:../click` is rejected like any unknown name.

Each cue is built in layers (a short transient, a body, and on `pop`, `chime`, `impact` and `ding` a reverb tail under 0.5 s) and every cue peaks at -6 dBFS in each channel. The tones a `note` sets stay at or below 19.2 kHz (0.4 × the 48 kHz rate): the FM attack click, the pitched partials and the low-note saturation, so a high `note` never folds back into noise. To stay under it, the FM attack click of `drop`, `pop` and `ding` gets duller as the note rises (from C3, F4 and C6, so `pop`'s default E5 is already a little dulled) and stops changing from E4, A5 and E7 up; `chime` drops its third partial from G#8 and `ding` its upper partial from A8; `impact`'s glide starts no higher than 19.2 kHz from D#8; and the saturation of `impact` and `drop` is left off from G#6 and G#7. Noise is not band-limited: `click`, `typing`, `whoosh`, `swoosh`, `riser` and the attack burst of `impact` do carry some energy above 19.2 kHz. The sounds are pure functions of the cue: noise is seeded, so the same spec gives the same WAV bytes and the same MP4. A cue longer than the time left is cut at the video end, and every cue is faded out over its last 5 ms in the mix so a cut one cannot click. With `scene`, `at` must be inside that scene (a smaller `at`, or a later scene, is the fix the validator names), so a cue always starts in the scene it names; it may still ring on into the next one (a `whoosh` started 0.4 s before a cut, a `riser` building into the next scene, a `chime` tail, or the last 0.05 s of a `typing` cue).

## Elements
Common: `id` (unique across scenes and elements), `type`, `x`, `y` (required; canvas pixels, except a group's children, whose `x`, `y` are relative to the group's centre), `opacity` (0–1, default 1), `scale` (1), `rotate` (deg, 0), `keyframes`, `hold` (boolean, false: this element may sit still for more than 3 s; on a group or a block it covers every child; see `check.mjs` below).

|---|---|---|
| `text` | `text` (req), `role` (`title` only; see below), `exit` (boolean, false; see below), `typing` (boolean, false; see below), `fontSize` (48), `fontWeight` 400\|500\|600\|700\|800 (600), `color` (the theme's text colour), `align` left\|center\|right (center), `maxWidth`, `font` sans\|mono | opacity x y scale rotate (+ `value` when `typing`) |
| `counter` | `to` (req), `from` (0), `decimals` (0), `prefix`, `suffix`, + text styling | + `value` (starts at `from`) |
| `shape` | `shape` rect\|ellipse\|line (req), `width`, `height`, `radius`, `fill`, `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `elevated` (rect only), `cap` and `arrow` (line only) | + `draw` 0–1 (stroke reveal; default 1) |
| `image` | `src` (req, relative to the spec file; must stay inside the spec folder, be a regular file, no symlinks out, no dotfiles), `width`, `height` (req), `fit` contain\|cover\|fill, `grade` (a colour grade; see Grade below) | opacity x y scale rotate |
| `video` | `src` (req; same path rules as image; `mp4` \| `mov` \| `webm` \| `m4v`), `width`, `height` (req), `fit` contain\|cover\|fill (contain), `trimStart` (s into the clip, 0), `trimEnd` (s into the clip, the clip length), `offset` (scene s before it plays, 0), `mute` (false), `volume` 0–1 (1), `grade` (see Grade below); see below | opacity x y scale rotate |

| `group` | `children` (req, 1–40 elements; see below) | opacity x y scale rotate |
| `block` | `block` (req, a block name), `params` (req, that block's fields); see Blocks below | opacity x y scale rotate (the whole block) |
| `code` | `code` (req, up to 10,000 characters; `\n` starts a line), `lang` (req: `text`, `javascript`, `typescript`, `json`, `bash`, `python`, `diff`, `go`, `rust`, `swift`, `yaml`, `html`, `css`, `markdown`), `width` (req), `height` (a minimum; longer code makes the box taller), `fontSize` (28), `highlight` (1-based line numbers), `diff` (boolean, false), `typing` (boolean, false; as on a text); see below | opacity x y scale rotate (+ `value` when `typing`) |

Group: `children` are ordinary elements (any type, a group too, up to 3 groups deep) whose `x` and `y` are measured from the **group's centre**, not the canvas: a group at `x: 960, y: 540` with a child at `x: 0, y: -100` puts the child at 960, 440. The group's `opacity`, `scale` and `rotate` act on all its children at once (scale and rotation turn about the group's centre), and its `x`/`y` keyframes move them together; each child keeps its own keyframes on top. A child's keyframe `t` is still seconds inside the scene. `id`s are unique across the whole spec, children included, and a validator problem in a child names its nested path (`scenes[0].elements[2].children[1].text`). `check.mjs` judges the children, not the group. A group fade is drawn as one layer, so it can differ from fading each child by one level per colour channel.

Video: real footage in a scene. The clip plays from `trimStart` to `trimEnd` starting `offset` seconds into its scene; before that it holds its first frame, and once the trimmed range ends it holds its last frame until the scene ends. It is timed by its own scene only (a scene change hides it like any element; under a `crossfade` into the next scene it stays frozen on its last frame of its own scene). Validation needs `ffprobe` (it ships with ffmpeg): it reads the clip's length, so `trimEnd` past it (`trimEnd 12 exceeds the clip length 8.5s`) and `trimStart` not before `trimEnd` (`trimStart must be less than trimEnd`) are errors; without ffprobe a spec with a video gets one error naming `doctor --fix ffmpeg`. **Render and check** decode the trimmed range once with ffmpeg (`-ss trimStart -to trimEnd`, `fps=<render fps>`, scaled to `width` x `height` by `fit`) into a frame cache, and the page shows frame `round((scene time - offset) × fps)` (held at the first and last) as an image, so every frame is exact and two renders of the same spec and clip are byte-identical. The frames are JPEG at the highest quality (PNG when the clip has an alpha channel), so the MP4 stays close to the source (measured on the 320x180 test clip in `test/video.test.mjs`: about 52 dB PSNR between the MP4 and an independent decode of the source). The cache lives in `<skill-dir>/.cache/video/<key>/` (gitignored), one folder per clip, keyed by the clip file's SHA-256, `trimStart`, `trimEnd`, the fps, the size, `fit`, alpha, the ffmpeg version and a cache format version, so a changed clip, field or ffmpeg decodes again and an unchanged one never does. A folder counts only when its `done.json` names a whole decode (a positive frame count, with the first and last frames present); anything else is decoded again. ffprobe and ffmpeg open clips and audio files through the `file` protocol only (`-protocol_whitelist file,pipe`), so a playlist renamed to `.mp4` cannot fetch anything. It grows with every new clip and size; `node scripts/doctor.mjs` lists it once it passes 2 GB, and `node scripts/doctor.mjs --fix cache` deletes it (any time; the next render decodes what it needs). The frames are decoded at the element's `width` x `height` in canvas pixels, so a `scale` keyframe above 1 or `render --size` larger than the spec's size enlarges them. **Sound:** unless `mute` is true, the clip's audio joins the mix like an `audio` track, from the moment it starts playing, at `volume`, for the trimmed length, and stops when its scene ends (the clip leaves the screen then, so its sound never plays over the next scenes); a clip without an audio stream adds nothing. **Preview** plays the clip itself in a muted `<video>` seeked to `trimStart + (scene time - offset)`: it is approximate (the nearest decodable frame, and it can lag while playing) and silent; the render is the exact one. Contact sheets and `check.mjs` use the decoded frames too, so they need ffmpeg as well.

Grade: `"grade": { "preset"?, "exposure"?, "contrast"?, "saturation"? }` on an `image` or a `video` (any other type is `grade: only valid on image or video elements`) colours the picture, not the element's box, so the element's own `opacity`, `scale`, `rotate` and `keyframes` act on the graded picture as before. `preset` is `warm`, `cool`, `mono` or `film`; `exposure` is in stops (−2 to 2, default 0; the picture is multiplied by 2^exposure), `contrast` 0.5 to 2 (default 1; it pivots on mid-grey, so 128 stays 128), `saturation` 0 to 2 (default 1). All three work without a preset. A grade that changes nothing (`{}`, or every number at its default and no preset) is an error that says what to set, and a value outside its range names the limit (`grade.exposure: must be <= 2`). A grade is not animatable: a keyframe cannot carry it. It is one SVG filter in the sRGB colour space (the 0–255 values a viewer sees), applied in this order: saturate by `saturation` × the preset's; the preset's colour matrix (warm and cool); then one linear step on red, green and blue with slope 2^`exposure` × `contrast` × the preset's contrast and intercept 0.5 − 0.5 × that contrast; then the preset's black lift (film). The presets: `warm` multiplies red by 1.08 (+0.02), green by 1.02 and blue by 0.9 (−0.02); `cool` multiplies red by 0.92 (−0.02), blue by 1.1 (+0.02); `mono` is saturation 0 whatever `saturation` says, with contrast × 1.1; `film` is saturation × 0.85, contrast × 1.15 and a table lift on each channel (`tableValues="0.06 1"`) after the linear step, so the floor holds at any exposure or contrast (measured through the page on greys: 0 reads 15, 128 reads 135, 255 stays 255). The page holds one `<svg><defs>` on the stage with one `<filter>` per graded element, named `grade-0`, `grade-1` and so on in document order: an element's own `id` never reaches the markup or a CSS `url()`. The filter sits on the `<img>` or `<video>` inside the element, outside every node the page renews, and adds no `will-change` in a render, so two renders of a graded spec are byte-identical, at any `--workers` (measured for an image and for a video in `test/grade.test.mjs`). `check.mjs` and the contact sheet screenshot the page, so a text over a graded photo is judged on the graded pixels. Example: `references/examples/graded-photo.json` (with `photo.png`, a landscape drawn procedurally for this repository: CC0).

Title role: a `text` with `"role": "title"` uses the theme's display font (the weight maps to the one the display font ships). `"font": "mono"` wins over the role: a mono title draws in the mono font, still with the entrance preset below. When it has **no** `keyframes` it gets the theme's kinetic entrance: `dusk` **rise** (fades in and rises 0.35 × `fontSize` into place, 0.9 s), `press` **ink-in** (fades in while settling from 104% scale, no movement, 1.1 s), `poster` **slam** (snaps in from 135% scale with an `outBack` landing, 0.5 s). A preset is a plain function of the scene duration and `fps`, compressed to end at least one frame before a short scene's end, so renders stay reproducible. Any `keyframes` (an empty list too) turn it off. `paper`, `ink`, `blueprint` and `editorial` have no preset.

Exit (opt-in): `"exit": true` on such a title also gives it the theme's exit, with the `exit` ease: `rise` fades as it drops 0.35 × `fontSize`, `ink-in` fades as it grows back to 104%, `slam` fades as it snaps down to 85%. The exit takes half the entrance (0.45 / 0.55 / 0.25 s) and lands one frame before the scene end, but never starts before 90% of the scene (a shorter scene gets a shorter exit, so the 85% contact frame always shows the title fully in). It is skipped on the spec's last scene, when the entrance has not landed by 90% of the scene, and whenever the title has its own `keyframes`. Without `exit` nothing changes.

Typing (opt-in): `"typing": true` on a `text` reveals its characters one at a time, 0.035 s each, from the start of its scene, with a caret after the last typed character (a thin bar; solid while typing, blinking every 0.5 s once the text is complete). Counted in grapheme clusters, so an emoji, a ZWJ sequence, a flag or a letter with a combining accent is one character and is never typed in halves. The element's box is always the full text: the untyped rest keeps its space but is not drawn, so a centred line does not shift as it fills and `check.mjs` judges its final box from the first frame. Its animatable `value` is the number of characters typed: with no `value` keyframes it runs 0 → N from t=0 at 0.035 s per character; add any `value` keyframe to take control of the start and pace (as on a counter's `value`). `typing` on any element type but a text or a code element is an error, and so is a `value` keyframe on a text without it. A typing text with no `value` keyframes must finish a frame before its scene ends (`0.035 × characters + 1/fps` seconds at most) and by the last frame the render draws of its scene, or the validator says to shorten the text or lengthen the scene to a length it names, one that passes. The render draws `round(total × fps)` frames at `i / fps` (a frame on a scene boundary is the next scene's), so a scene's last frame can come up to 1.5 frames before its end; the same rule holds for typing code and for the blocks with a schedule (`code-typing`, `terminal-run`, `listicle`, `story-beats`, `before-after-swipe`).

Code: highlighted source code in the theme's mono font, left-aligned in a box `width` wide, one row per line (a long line wraps at any character inside the width, so the box never grows sideways). It is highlighted once at load with [shiki](https://shiki.style) (MIT): its fine-grained core, its JavaScript regex engine (no WebAssembly) and only the grammars the spec's code elements name, all served from the skill's own `node_modules`, so nothing is fetched. Colours come from the theme's dark tokens: keywords `accent`, strings `good`, comments `text3`, numbers and constants `warn`, functions `cat1`, types `cat2`, everything else `text`; the background is transparent. Each `highlight` line gets a `surface` row behind it. With `"diff": true` a line starting with `+` gets a `good` tint (15 %), one starting with `-` a `bad` tint, and that first character is drawn as a marker, the rest highlighted as `lang`. `typing` types the code exactly as a text types (0.035 s a character, newlines counted, the caret moving row to row; the box is the whole code from the first frame), with the same rule: no `value` keyframes means it must finish a frame before the scene ends (`scenes[0].elements[0].code: typing 100 characters takes 3.50s … shorten the code or lengthen the scene …`). The code is drawn as text, never as markup: `<script>` in it is just those characters. An unknown `lang` names the closest one (`scenes[0].elements[0].lang: unknown lang "js"; did you mean "javascript"? (langs: text, javascript, …)`), and a `highlight` past the end says how many lines there are (`highlight[1]: line 5 is past the end; the code has 3 lines`). `check.mjs` judges a code element like a text: safe area, overlap, caption zone, text floor (28 px at 1080, so the default size passes exactly) and contrast, where every token colour is judged against the backdrop and the worst one reported.

Counter default: a counter with **no** `value` keyframes counts `from` → `to` from t=0 to `min(2, 0.8 × scene duration)` seconds with `outCubic`. Add any `value` keyframe to take control of the timing.

Colours (`background`, scene `background` other than `"art"`, `color`, `fill`, `stroke`) must be hex (`#rgb`, `#rrggbb`, with optional alpha), `rgb()/rgba()/hsl()/hsla()` or a named CSS colour. `url(...)`, `var(...)` and anything else is rejected.

Theme colour tokens: a shape `fill` or `stroke` and a text or counter `color` may also name a token (`surface`, `line`, `accent`, `accentSoft`, `good`, `bad`, `warn`, `text`, `text2`, `text3`, `cat1`..`cat6`) instead of a hex. It resolves to that colour of the active theme's dark variant, so a theme change recolours the card too. Backgrounds take no tokens; an unknown name is an error that lists them. Hex specs render exactly as before.

Shape notes: a `line` has length `width`, thickness `strokeWidth` and is horizontal; angle it with `rotate`. To grow a vertical bar upward from a baseline, use a line with `"rotate": 270` and animate `draw` 0→1. `fill` defaults to the theme accent for rect/ellipse unless only a `stroke` is given. `"elevated": true` on a rect draws a card: the fill moves 6 % toward the theme text colour and a 1 px `rgba(255,255,255,.06)` highlight sits just inside the edge (no drop shadow, which would not show on a dark page); it is drawn as one extra node after the rect, is drawn in with it as `draw` grows, and is left off a rect with only a `stroke` (no fill to lift). `"elevated": false` is accepted on any shape and does nothing. `"cap": "round"` rounds a line's ends and pulls both endpoints in by half the stroke width, so the line keeps its `width`; the default stays flat. A round-cap line stays hidden until its drawn length is more than a cap radius (half the stroke width), so it grows from a stub rather than popping in as a dot. A line with an arrow needs a `width` of more than 3 × `strokeWidth` (3.5 × with a round cap) and one with a round cap alone more than `strokeWidth`; the validator names the minimum. `"arrow": "end"` adds a filled triangle head (3 × `strokeWidth` long, 3 × wide, ending at `width`) that appears once `draw` reaches 0.98.

Chart notes: data must be inline: `data.values`, or `datasets` plus `data: {name}`. Any `url` key anywhere in the Vega-Lite spec (remote `data.url`, lookup `from.data.url`, image-mark `url`) is rejected; the renderer also blocks all non-local network requests. Do not set `width`/`height` inside `vegaLite`; the element's are used. The theme's fonts, axis colours, accent and palette are applied through a built-in Vega-Lite `config`; your own `config` keys override it. The built-in config also sets: about five ticks on each axis, no y domain line, no tick marks and no vertical gridlines, bars with 10 px rounded ends and a 0.3 gap between them, lines 4 px wide with round caps and joins, and filled points ringed in the page colour (a hollow `filled: false` point keeps its accent outline). The chart `reveal` is a left-to-right wipe with a soft edge. The chart is rendered to SVG once at load.

## Blocks
A `block` element is a parameterised building block: `{ "id": "speaker", "type": "block", "x": 100, "y": 818, "block": "lower-third", "params": { "name": "Ada Lovelace", "role": "Head of Research" } }`. Before anything else reads the spec it expands into a `group` with the block's `id`, `x`, `y`, `opacity`, `scale`, `rotate`, `keyframes` and `hold`, holding plain elements named `<id>/<part>` (`speaker/name`, `steps/box-2`). The parts are laid out for the canvas the spec resolves to (its `size`, else its `format`'s), sized from the short side, coloured with theme tokens only (`surface`, `line`, `accent`, `text`, `text2`, `good`), and enter with the `enter` ease over 0.6 s, 0.2 s apart; in a short scene the whole schedule is compressed so nothing moves after 85 % of the scene. Every text is at least 40 px at 1080 on the short side. The floor is relative to the short side, so it scales with the canvas: a 16:9 video shrunk to a phone's width shows that text small (about 7 px on the 360 px phone tile of a 1920-wide sheet), while at 9:16 it fills the phone. Use `format: "9:16"` for phone-first video; a text that would not fit shrinks to that floor, then wraps; sizes are estimated from the characters, since expansion runs without a page, so the room left around a text is generous. A block goes directly in a scene's `elements`, not inside a group. `node scripts/blocks.mjs list` prints every block's params, and `node scripts/blocks.mjs show <name>` prints one block's params table, its place on each format and an example element. `references/blocks/<name>.png` is the contact sheet of `references/examples/blocks/<name>.json`, for human review (CI never compares it: fonts rasterise differently per OS).

## Keyframes
`{ "t": seconds, "ease": name, <prop>: value, ... }`
- `t` is relative to the scene start, sorted ascending, ≤ scene duration.
- Per property the track is built from the keyframes that set it: before the first, the value is that keyframe's value; between two, it interpolates using the **destination** keyframe's `ease` (default `linear`); after the last, it holds. Properties no keyframe sets keep the element's base value.
- Sampling: frame *i* shows time *i / fps*, so the last frame is one frame **before** the scene/video end. Put the final keyframe at least one frame (1/fps s) before the scene end, or it is never shown fully.
- `ease`: `linear`, or `in|out|inOut` × `Quad|Cubic|Quart|Back|Elastic|Bounce|Sine|Expo|Circ`, or a named ease: `enter` (cubic-bezier 0.16, 1, 0.3, 1: fast out, soft settle, for entrances), `exit` (0.6, 0, 0.9, 0.35: slow start, accelerating away), `move` (0.65, 0, 0.35, 1: symmetric, for a move between two places), `land` (0.55, 0, 0.2, 1.12: overshoots about 1% just before it lands; one hero moment per clip; never on a counter's `value`, where the number would show past its target). See `motion-design.md`.

## Captions
Optional root `captions` array, up to 20 tracks: the words of a captions file on screen, one group at a time, centred in the caption zone (see `format`; the lower part of the safe area). Opt-in: a spec with no `captions` renders exactly as before.
| field | type | default | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `src` | string | required | relative to the spec file; same path rules as image `src`; `srt`, `vtt` or `json`. A JSON file is Whisper output (`segments[].words[]` or a top-level `words` array, each `{ word` or `text`, `start, end }` in seconds; segments without `words` are timed like cues) or the voice sidecar `voice.mjs` writes (`{ sentences: [{ text, start, end }] }`) |
| `style` | `plain` \| `pill` \| `karaoke` \| `kinetic` \| `boxed` \| `typewriter` | `plain` | `plain`: the text with a soft page-colour shadow; `pill`: on a rounded backdrop of the theme `surface` at 0.85 alpha; `karaoke`: the word being spoken in the theme accent; `kinetic`: the word being spoken in the theme accent colour, popping from 1.15x to its size over its first 0.15 s (less when a bigger pop would take the word out of the caption zone: a word as wide as the zone does not pop); `boxed`: on a square-cornered `surface` backdrop at 0.85 alpha; `typewriter`: each group types in, one character every 0.035 s |
| `at` | seconds ≥ 0 | 0 | where the file's time 0 falls: from the video start; with `scene`, from that scene's start. Only one track may be on screen at a time: two tracks whose groups overlap before the video ends are an error (`captions[1]: on screen at 1.50s together with captions[0]; …`) |
| `scene` | scene `id` | none | anchors `at` to the scene, as on an audio track; give a voiceover's captions the same `scene` and `at` as its audio track so they stay together |

**Timing.** An SRT or VTT cue is split into words at spaces; each word gets a share of the cue's time in proportion to its characters plus one for the space after it (none for the last word), and the last word ends at the cue's end. A voice sidecar sentence and a Whisper segment without words are timed the same way; Whisper words are used as given. A cue must start after the one before it (`cue 3 (line 9): start 00:00:01,000 is not after the previous cue's start 00:00:02,000; sort the cues by time`), and a cue that runs past the next one's start is cut there. A cue with no words is skipped. Only tag-shaped markup is dropped (`<i>`, `<b>`, `<u>`, `<font …>`, `<c.class>`, `<v Name>`, `<lang …>`, `<ruby>`, `<rt>`, karaoke timestamps such as `<00:00:01.000>`, and `{\an8}` overrides) and `<br>` / `<br/>` becomes a space, so `a<b and c>d` stays as written; `&amp;`, `&lt;`, `&gt;`, `&quot;`, `&apos;`, `&nbsp;`, `&#NNN;` and `&#xHH;` are decoded. In an SRT file a second timing line inside a block starts a new cue (the blank line before it was missing; a cue number just above it goes with it). A UTF-8 byte-order mark is ignored in every format. With the voice sidecar the sentence edges are exact but word times inside a sentence are these estimates, so `karaoke` and `kinetic` from a sidecar are approximate; Whisper word timings are exact, and aligning a synthesised voiceover word by word is not built in.

**Grouping** (HyperFrames' caption rules). One group is on screen at a time, 2 to 6 words and at most 42 characters (an emoji or a CJK character counts as one; a word is never split, so one longer word is a group of its own). A new group starts after a word that ends a sentence (ending in `.`, `!`, `?`, `。`, `！`, `？`, `…` or `‽`, also before closing quotes or brackets, as in `"go."` or `(done!)`), after a pause of 0.15 s or more, at 6 words, or before a word that would take the group past 42 characters. A one-word group then joins the next group when it is at most 0.5 s away and the result keeps to 6 words and 42 characters, else the previous group under the same conditions, else it takes the adjacent word of a neighbour that can spare one, never a word that ends a sentence (so `one two three four five six seven` shows as five words, then two); a word with no group within 0.5 s, or a one-word track, stays alone. Each group ends when the next starts, or at its last word's end. Nothing shows before 0.1 s: groups that end by then are dropped and the first one left starts at 0.1 s at the earliest; a track that would show nothing at all is an error (`nothing would show: every word ends by 0.1 s into the video; …`).

**Look.** One layer per track, over the scenes and under `texture`, so it does not fade or crossfade with a scene. The text is the theme's body font at its shipped weight nearest 700, 48 px at 1080 on the short side (48 px at 1920x1080 and 1080x1920), in the theme's text colour. The layer is at most the zone's width; a long group wraps onto balanced lines (a 42-character group takes at most two), a word too long for a line breaks anywhere, and a group taller than the zone is shrunk to fit it. A number with its unit stays on one line, as in a text element. `check.mjs` fails on a visible text or counter held in the caption zone while a caption is on screen, and on a captions layer outside the zone (see CLI).

- `node scripts/check.mjs <spec> [--json] [--theme name]` — seeks the render page to every contact-sheet moment (all of them, uncapped); flags a visible text or counter outside the safe area (60 px at 1080 on the short side, on every side: 60 px at 1920x1080, 120 px at 3840x2160) while it is not moving (between or after its keyframe tweens), two visible texts or counters overlapping while neither is moving or fading, and a scene that ends with nothing on screen (every element invisible or wholly off the canvas; a title whose `exit` preset was applied counts as present, since it left on purpose). With `captions`, also a visible text or counter whose box reaches into the caption zone while it is not moving and a caption is on screen (`scenes[0].elements[1]: text "sub" sits in the caption zone (y >= 842) at 1.00s; move it above y 842 or end the captions before it`; with no caption showing, the lower band is free; judged at the sheet moments and at every caption group's midpoint), and a captions layer that leaves the zone (`captions[0]: captions leave the caption zone (right) at 1.40s`, video time). Each layer is measured once per caption group, at its midpoint (and for `kinetic` at each word's start, where the pop is largest), as the box around the layer and every word, so transforms and the pop count. A **warning**, never a failure (exit stays 0; printed to stderr as `warning: scenes[0]: ...`, and under `warnings` in `--json`, which is left out when there is none), when the first scene shows nothing at t = 0, since that blank frame is the video's thumbnail; the message names the cause (a `fade`, an opacity 0 first keyframe such as a title's theme entrance, a line or chart at `draw`/`reveal` 0, or a typing text or code element with no characters typed yet: its caret alone is not content). An `art` background counts as shown. A scene is judged where it settles, never where a `push`, `slide`, `zoom`, `wipe`, `blur` or `iris` window has moved, clipped or blurred it. Each transition window is sampled at its midpoint, with both layers drawn as they are, and a visible text or counter of the incoming scene that overlaps one of the outgoing scene there is a warning too (`warning: scenes[1].elements[0]: text "b" overlaps text "a" of the outgoing scene "a" during the crossfade at 2.20s; ...`; a `push` moves the layers apart, so it has none). A box wholly off the canvas has left the frame and is never clipped. A scene `camera` is part of the box (boxes are read after it), and a camera tween counts as every element of the scene moving, (see Camera). Four audits sample every scene on a 0.25 s grid (and the colour behind each text at the sheet moments): **contrast**, WCAG AA of a text's or counter's colour (a `code` element's worst token colour) against the pixels behind it (4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text: 24 px at 1080 on the short side, or 18.67 px at weight 700 or more). The text is made transparent and one screenshot read, so the art-background plate counts as backdrop; the text box is split into up to 4 x 2 cells and the worst cell is judged, with the colour blended at its alpha (`rgba(255,255,255,0.15)`) and the element's opacity. A text inside an opacity tween of its own or a group's is skipped at that moment. `scenes[0].elements[1]: text "sub" has 2.1:1 contrast against its backdrop at 0.10s; needs 3:1; nearest passing token: bad` (the first of `text`, `text2`, `text3`, `accent`, then the other colour tokens that passes over every cell; when none does, `no single colour passes over this background; add a backing card (fill "surface") or move the text`); **reading floor**, a `text` stays on screen for 0.75 x (0.5 + 0.3 x words) seconds (7 words need 1.95 s, the last line included; a text is reported when its measured time on screen is more than one 0.25 s step short, the edges of a borderline span being measured with extra page reads; the need is printed rounded up; a last line still shown at the final frame for under 2 s that clears the formula gets an **info** line instead, `info: scenes[0].elements[1]: text "bye" (1 word) shows for 1.50s; a last line reads best at 2 s or more`, printed on stderr and under `info` in `--json`, never changing the exit code), `text "sub" (7 words) shows for 1.25s; needs 1.95s`; **dead stretch**, more than 3 s in which nothing on screen changes (an element's position, size, opacity, text, drawing, reveal, value or video frame, a caption, a scene cut, a transition window), except in the last 3 s of the video and while only `hold` elements are on screen, `scenes[0]: nothing changes from 4.00s to 7.50s (3.5s); add motion, or set "hold": true on text "a", rect "panel" if they are meant to sit still` (only elements that are shown count, so an invisible or off-canvas drifting element hides nothing; the message names the shown elements without `hold`, and a block once as `block "lt"` rather than its parts, since `hold` goes on the block); **text floor**, a `text`, `counter` or `code` drawn under 28 px at 1080 on the short side (scaled by the canvas, by `scale` and by a scene camera's zoom) for 0.5 s or more while its own size is not tweening (a camera zoom tween does not relax it), `text "fine-print" is 22px (below 28px at 1080); shown 1.50s`. A contrast or text-floor finding at a single sampled moment is a **warning** (stderr, exit stays 0), at two or more consecutive moments a problem; reading floor and dead stretch are always problems. Captions are not elements, so the reading floor and text floor never judge them (their timing comes from the transcript), though a caption change counts as change for the dead-stretch audit. A finding inside a block names the block, the part and what to change (contrast: `blocks use theme colours; change "theme" or the scene background`, or when no colour passes `no theme colour passes over this background; change "theme" or the scene background`; text floor: `raise the block's "scale" or use a larger format`; reading floor: a longer scene or a shorter param). The contrast audit reads no backdrop inside the window of a transition the scene comes in through (any type, `crossfade` included: the screenshot is two layers there; and a `fade`'s, where the scene is half faded in), so a text is judged at its later moments; a text whose every shown moment falls inside its window is not judged at all and gets `info: scenes[1].elements[0]: text "x" was not judged for contrast: every moment it shows in falls inside the push window of its scene; lengthen the scene or shorten the transition` (printed on stderr and under `info` in `--json`, never changing the exit code). `--theme` is the theme for a spec that sets none, as in render.mjs (an unknown name exits 2). A crash prints `check crashed: …` alone (JSON `crashed: true`). Exit 0 ok (or `check: skipped (no browser: …)`), 1 problems, 2 usage.
- `node scripts/render.mjs <spec> [--out file.mp4] [--fps n] [--size WxH] [--theme name] [--model m] [--effort e] [--agent a] [--workers n|auto] [--quality draft|final] [--loudness <lufs>|off] [--true-peak <dbtp>] [--contact-sheet]` — `--workers` (an integer 1–16, or `auto` = max(1, min(8, cores − 1)); default 1) captures frames on that many pages of one browser (never more pages than frames): the pages take the next frame in turn and ffmpeg receives the frames in order. A frame's bytes do not depend on which page drew it or on which frames were drawn before it, so the MP4 is byte-identical to `--workers 1`; audio is mixed once after the frames as before, and a page that fails stops the render with `worker <k> failed at frame <j>: <message>` (k counts from 0; exit 1, no partial MP4). Frames wait as PNG files in a temp folder outside the spec folder, removed on every exit. A page never runs more than 2 frames per page ahead of the encoder, so at most 2 × workers PNG files exist at once (8 workers: 16 files, about 0.5 MB at 1080p for a flat design, about 10 MB with film grain) however slow the encoder is. Each page is a full copy of the stage in memory, and a scene with `texture.grain` holds a full-frame grain bitmap on each page, so memory grows with `--workers`: at 4K with grain and many scenes, keep it low (`auto` is capped at 8). `--quality draft` renders at half the size on each side (rounded down to even, never under 16; 1920x1080 gives 960x540) with `-crf 30 -preset veryfast`: the page itself is that size, so captions, the safe area and fonts shrink with it; `final` is the default and changes nothing. `workers: N` (only when N is above 1) and `quality: draft (WxH)` go to stderr; stdout is the same either way. An unknown value (`--workers 0`, `--workers 17`, `--quality fast`) exits 2 and lists the accepted ones, as do `--quality draft --contact-sheet` and `--workers <anything> --contact-sheet` (a contact sheet is always full size, on one page, whatever the core count). `--model`/`--effort`/`--agent` override the spec's `generatedBy` (hostile values fail validation, exit 1); `--theme` is a shared theme for a spec that sets none (a spec's own `theme` wins; an unknown name exits 2). Default out `<specdir>/out/<name>.mp4`; an `--out` that is a file the render reads (the spec, or a clip, image, sound or captions file it names) exits 2 before anything is written (`--out: clip.mp4 is a file this render reads (footage/clip.mp4); choose another --out`); with `--contact-sheet`, `--out` is a directory and PNGs go to `<out>/contact/<scene-id>.png` (default `<specdir>/out`). `--size` sides must be 16–3840 and even. Output is written to `<name>.partial.mp4` and renamed on success; Ctrl+C removes it (exit 130/143). `--loudness <lufs>` (−30 to −5) normalises the mix to that integrated loudness and `--true-peak <dbtp>` (−3 to 0) sets the true-peak ceiling (−1.5 when neither the flag nor the spec's `loudness` gives one); without a flag the spec's root `loudness` is the target (viral mode sets −14 / −1), a flag winning field by field, and `--loudness off` turns normalising off even when the spec sets one. After the encode (into `<name>.partial.mp4`), a first `loudnorm` pass (`I`, `TP`, `LRA=11`, `print_format=json`) measures the mix and a second copies the video stream and re-encodes the audio through a linear-mode `loudnorm` with those measurements (AAC 48 kHz stereo 192 kb/s as before) into a second temp beside it (`<name>.partial.loudnorm.mp4`), capped at the clip length (`-t`) as the first encode is, so the audio is not a frame longer than the video; that temp is renamed onto the output, and an error or Ctrl+C removes both temps. A mix one gain cannot take is told by pass 1: the gain to the target would put its peaks over the true-peak ceiling (`loudnorm` would cap the gain and land short of the target), or its loudness range is over 11 LU (`loudnorm` would fall back to its dynamic mode). Then the gain is applied first, a lookahead `alimiter` holds the peaks at the ceiling minus 3 dB (`volume=<gain>dB,alimiter=limit=<TP-3 dB>:level=false:latency=true`), a pass 1b measures that limited signal with the range check lifted (`LRA=50`), and the same linear `loudnorm` finishes it. loudnorm's measure-only passes read a wide-range signal up to 1.4 LU louder than the 48 kHz chain pass 2 runs, so the finished chain is dry-run once and measured with `ebur128` at 48 kHz, and a miss of 0.1 dB or more is corrected with a `volume` after loudnorm (never past the ceiling). Measured on the signals tried (cue mixes, and synthetic wide-range piano and stepped-dynamics signals) the result lands within 1 LU of the target under the ceiling and is byte-identical twice; that is a measurement, not a guarantee for every signal. Mixes that take this branch: those of cues alone whose peaks the gain would push over the ceiling or whose range is over 11 LU (a default viral clip, `viral-hook.json` included), and any music or voice track with a loudness range over 11 LU, which used to meet loudnorm's dynamic mode. A tone, music or voice mix that one gain can reach (peak plus gain under the ceiling, range 11 LU or less) never takes it, and its bytes are those of the plain two passes. The limiter reduces the peaks by `peak + gain − (ceiling − 3 dB)`; when that is more than 6 dB, stderr says `loudness: the limiter reduces this mix's peaks by up to N dB (more than 6 dB) to reach -14 LUFS; the mix is quiet or peaky for that target, so consider raising its level or lowering the target`. The stage needs ffmpeg 5.1 or later (alimiter's `latency` option and an LRA over 20 first appear there; audio mixing alone still needs 4.4). Whether it can run is asked of the ffmpeg itself, not read from its version: one dry run of the stage's own filters on 0.1 s of silence, once per process (a git build or a wrapper that hides its version is judged the same way). On an ffmpeg that fails it, a mix that needs the stage is not given to loudnorm at all (from 4.4 to 5.0 its cap on an input under 3 s is a difference of two amplitudes, fixed in 6.0, which makes a short clip quieter) but a plain `volume` gain, `min(target − input_i, ceiling − input_tp)` from pass 1, so the true peak stays at or under the ceiling and the clip is never made quieter; stderr says `loudness: this mix needs the limiter stage to reach the target, which needs ffmpeg 5.1 or later (this is 4.4); it is normalised with a plain gain capped at the true-peak ceiling, so it may read off target`, and the render does not fail. `node scripts/doctor.mjs` shows the installed version beside the 5.1 requirement as a note, never a failure. The `limiter reduces` figure is never negative (0 when the gain leaves the peaks under the limit). A spec with no audio ignores the flag (stderr: `loudness: this spec has no audio, so --loudness and --true-peak are ignored`), a silent mix is left as it is (stderr note), an `--out` whose temp file names (`<name>.partial.mp4`, and with a target `<name>.partial.loudnorm.mp4`) leave a name over 255 bytes exits 2 with the limit before the browser starts (a batch row: `row 0: --out: …`, exit 1, nothing rendered; `--loudness off` shortens the longer name), `--true-peak` with no target (no `--loudness`, no spec field) or with `--loudness off` exits 2, and so does a value outside the ranges, with the range in the message; neither flag goes with `--contact-sheet`. In a `--batch` the same applies to every row and the manifest's `sha256` is of the final normalised file. The result is byte-identical twice, with `--workers` and `--quality draft` as without; a spec with no target renders exactly as before. When the spec has `audio`, the MP4 also carries an AAC 48 kHz stereo track at 192 kb/s, mixed with normalisation off so `volume` is literal; the video length never changes because of audio. Before any frame is captured each audio file gets a decode probe on its first audio stream (a bad or video-only file fails with `audio[i].src: ffmpeg cannot decode …`, exit 1), and the whole mix graph is dry-run for 0.1 s against a generated video (an ffmpeg older than 4.4 fails with `audio: ffmpeg rejected the mix …`, exit 1). Every track is converted to 48 kHz stereo before mixing, so track order never changes the result. A spec without `audio` or `cues` renders exactly as before, with no audio stream. After any render that has audio, a line `audio: -17.6 LUFS integrated, true peak -3.3 dBTP` (ffmpeg `ebur128`) follows the frame count: information only, it never fails a render. `--fps` is an output override (integer 1–120); the spec's own `fps` is limited to 24/25/30/60. Two renders of the same spec and media give identical frames and audio (at any `--workers`). A frame is the same bytes whether it follows a walk through every earlier frame or a seek straight to its time, so a contact-sheet tile and `check.mjs` draw each element the way the MP4 does (Chromium redraws an element from a memory of how it looked, so the page renews each element whose position, scale, rotation or opacity changed in a seek, and every element under a moving camera; see `renewNodes` in `template/renderer.mjs`); the MP4 `comment` tag carries the build date, so the files differ by day unless `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` is set.
- `node scripts/render.mjs <spec> --batch <rows.json> --out "<dir>/<name with {index} or {var}>.mp4" [--fps n] [--size WxH] [--theme name] [--workers n|auto] [--quality draft|final] [--loudness lufs|off] [--true-peak dbtp] [--model m] [--effort e] [--agent a]` — one MP4 per row of a rows file: a JSON array of objects, each giving var values (`[{ "name": "Grace", "seats": 3 }, { "name": "Alan" }]`; a var the row leaves out takes its `default`). A key that is no declared var is an extra column: it is ignored, with a warning on stderr that names the closest var (`row 2: unknown column "cty" ignored; declared: city, name (did you mean "city"?)`), and never fails the batch. Every value of a declared var must be of its type, never coerced (`row 1: vars.seats: the row gives "12abc" (a string); seats is a number`), and a var with no default needs a value in every row. `--out` must hold `{index}` (the row's place, from 0) or `{<var>}` in its file name (`--batch needs an --out template with {index} or a {var}, so outputs do not collide`, exit 2), and the file name must end in `.mp4`; placeholders or any braces in the folder part as written (a folder written in `--out` must not contain `{` or `}`; the working folder may), an unknown name, or the spec's `{{name}}` form (`--out: write placeholders with single braces, like {name}`) exit 2 too, and so does a `{name}` placeholder in the file name of `--out` without `--batch` (a folder named `{draft}` is fine there). A value put in a file name keeps only letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-` (each other run becomes `-`, dots and dashes at the ends go, 80 characters at most), so no row writes outside the `--out` folder; a value that leaves nothing (`""`, `/`, `..`) two rows that would write one file (compared ignoring case, `ß` as `ss`), and a file name over 255 bytes (with its temporary prefix and suffix) are errors, and so is an output that is a file the batch reads: the spec, the rows file, or any row's image, clip, sound or captions file (`row 0: --out: clip.mp4 is a file this batch reads (footage/clip.mp4); change the value or the --out template`). A number value loses its sign in a file name (`-5` gives `5`). Every row is substituted and checked before the first frame (the spec's own checks, `--fps` cut-offs and the output file), every row's failures printed as `row 2: scenes[0].elements[1].src: must stay inside the spec folder`, exit 1, nothing rendered; a row whose var values are wrong (a missing value, a wrong type) reports those alone, since the spec cannot be substituted without them, and its other failures show once they are fixed. Then the rows render one after another, each with its own browser and the same flags (`--workers`, `--quality`, audio, cues and captions per row): `row <i>: <out>` then the usual lines. Each row encodes into a hidden temp file of its own (`.<name>.<index>.partial.mp4`, which no value can produce; anything already at that name, a symlink included, is removed and the file created anew just before ffmpeg opens it by name, so a link left there beforehand is replaced, not written through, though a link planted in the instant between the two would be followed; the manifest's temp file is created exclusively, so it is never written through a link) and is renamed onto its output only when complete, so an older file under a row's name is replaced when the row succeeds and left as it was when it fails (the manifest's `status` says which: never trust a `failed` row's `out`). A row that fails while rendering is recorded and its temp file removed; the rest still render, and the exit is 1. After the last row every `ok` row is checked again (its file must exist and match its `sha256`), so the manifest never vouches for a file that changed. `manifest.json` in the `--out` folder (rewritten after every row: a temp file, then a rename; a `manifest.json` already there that is not a batch manifest, such as a web app's, is never overwritten: exit 2 before anything renders; a batch manifest is an object whose `rows` each have an integer `index` and a `status` of `ok` or `failed`) holds `{ "rows": [{ "index", "vars", "status": "ok" | "failed", "out", "sha256", "duration", "error"? }] }`: `vars` the values used (defaults filled in), `out` the file name, `sha256` of the MP4 (null when failed), `duration` the video's seconds. A rows file that is not a non-empty JSON array of objects exits 2. `--batch` with `--contact-sheet` exits 2. `--model`, `--effort` and `--agent` go into `generatedBy` after the vars are substituted, so a `{{name}}` in them is never replaced. An unknown option exits 2 and lists the accepted ones.
- ffmpeg is looked up as `$FFMPEG_PATH`, then `ffmpeg` on PATH, then an optionally installed `ffmpeg-static`. Audio mixing needs ffmpeg ≥ 4.4 (`amix normalize`, `adelay all`); an older build fails at encode time with `Option normalize not found`.
